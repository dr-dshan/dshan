Emerging Materials for Symmetry-Driven Control

Use crystal and interface symmetry to uncover new electronic, spin and chiral responses and turn them into ways to control devices.

Research Focus

Find responses allowed by crystal and interface symmetry, then test how to control them.

Symmetry and band degeneracy

Combined inversion and time reversal can keep two bands at the same energy at each momentum.

Weyl metals and low-symmetry crystals

We study how crystal symmetry and band topology shape the generation and transport of spin and orbital angular momentum, and how these currents can be used for electrical readout and magnetization control.

Altermagnets and compensated order

Altermagnets combine compensated antiferromagnetic order with momentum-dependent spin splitting. We explore electrical readout through spin-dependent transport and writing through current-induced torques. The goal is to replace the ferromagnetic storage layer in MRAM with a compensated layer that can switch ultrafast and better tolerate stray magnetic fields.

Chirality in bulk and interfaces

Study how structural handedness or broken inversion symmetry can select a rotation direction. In layered magnets, this can appear as chiral exchange or a preferred domain-wall rotation.

From symmetry to control

Vary crystal orientation, layer stacking and interface composition, then look for transport or optical signals that distinguish the response.

Impact

Symmetry tells us which responses can be generated and how to distinguish them in measurements. Using that information to choose a material, orientation or interface can open new ways to control or read out electronic and spin states.
