In-Memory Computing for AI Inference

MRAM stores neural-network weights and uses those same cells to perform the multiply-accumulate operations behind AI inference.

Research Focus

Store neural-network weights in MRAM and compute dot products inside the array.

Why compute in memory?

Inference repeatedly fetches weights to calculate dot products. Computing inside the array reduces those transfers and sends scores to the next stage.

Why MRAM?

Magnetic tunnel junctions retain weights without power, support repeated writes, and offer two resistance states for binary weights. Low cell resistance can draw substantial current in a conventional current-summing crossbar.

How the MRAM array computes

The input selects an MTJ path to encode a match or mismatch. A sense circuit reads the sum of resistances in each column as a dot-product score.

What we evaluate

Compare inference accuracy, energy and latency, including the input drivers, sensing circuits and any weight reprogramming.

Impact

Multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations are at the core of neural network inference. Storing weights in nonvolatile MRAM and performing MAC operations inside the array can reduce the need to move weights between memory and compute units, especially in power-constrained edge devices. The practical benefit depends on the energy and accuracy of the full circuit, including sensing and conversion.
